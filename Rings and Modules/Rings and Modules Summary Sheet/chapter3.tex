\chapter{Ideals and Quotients}
\begin{definition}
    $x \in  R$ non-zero, not a unit is irreducible if whenever $a | x$ we have $a \sim 1$ or $a \sim x$.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}
    If every ideal in integral domain $R$ is principal, we say $R$ is a Principal Ideal Domain (PID).
\end{definition}
\begin{eg}
    $\Z, \F[t]$
\end{eg}
If we're asked to show a quotient is isomorphic to something, a good way to do it is to produce a SURJECTION onto the something whose kernel is the thing we're quotienting out by and using the isomorphism theorem.
\begin{prop}
    
If the only ideals in commutative ring $R$ are $\{0\} $ and $R$, then $R$ is a field.
\end{prop}
